This section will cover some of the prerequisite knowledge, that will put the real nerdy stuff, further down, into context. At least a little bit.

A black hole is a region in space where everything is squished so hard together by gravity, that neutron degeneracy pressure is beaten. This is basically the universe's last and strongest barrier, that say; "HEY! You can't have stuff at the same place at the same time!".
The next question, which is totally on everyone's mind;
"So if this neutron pressure stuff just needs to be overpowered for something to collapse into a black hole, does that mean I can become one?".
Yes. You'd be a tiny black hole, that'd evaporate practically instantly, but a black hole nonetheless.

Now let's go through some terms that'll probably some up, somewhere in here.

Event Horizon: A border, surrounding the black hole, beyond which the gravitaional pull of the black hole becomes too strong for anything to escape. This also includes photons.

Photon: A light wave/particle. They carry no mass/weight and it they're the fastest "thing" in the universe.

√27 × M

The border of the black hole itself is not the event horizon. The black hole's border, the one you can see, is larger than the event horizon. This is because, anything that passes within about 2.6 times that radius still curves back in eventually, even if it never touches the horizon. So the black you're seeing is less "the hole itself" and more the no-escape zone around it.

1 equation

Every pixel on your screen is a photon, traced backward from your eye through curved space. One equation decides how much each one bends: acceleration scales with the photon's angular momentum squared, divided by distance to the fifth power. The poor GPU runs that calculation 300 times per pixel, every single frame.

The ring

The bright ring is mostly real physics: photons that skim close to 1.5x the horizon radius get whipped almost all the way around before escaping, dragging a smeared, compressed image of the whole sky with them. Some of the noise you see right at the edge is the shader being messy, not the universe — fix incoming. The ring itself is modelled after the same effect that let astronomers photograph M87* and Sagittarius A*, the first actual black hole images ever taken.

G = c = 1

The cool physicists set the gravitational constant and the speed of light to 1, just to strip the clutter out of the equations. Standard move, not a cheat. The "toy-units" in the readouts map back to real kilometers through the Schwarzschild radius (Rs = 2GM/c²) — a 5 solar mass black hole works out to about 14.8 km.
