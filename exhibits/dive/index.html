Mass-shell condition
H = ½ g^μν p_μ p_ν = −½
Light travels on paths where H = 0. Anything with mass travels on paths where H = −½. Same Hamiltonian, same integrator, one different number — that's the entire difference between the photons this shader traces and the observer tracing them.
Kerr-Schild radius
r² = ½[(ρ²−a²) + √((ρ²−a²)² + 4a²z²)]

Surfaces of constant r are flattened ellipsoids, not spheres. These coordinates stay finite across the horizon, which is why the fall can be integrated straight through it instead of stopping there.

H = −½

The camera isn't following a hand-drawn path. It's a particle with mass, dropped in from twelve horizon radii out, integrated through the Kerr metric with the same Hamiltonian machinery the shader uses on photons. The only difference between the observer and the light it sees is one number on the right-hand side. Whatever the camera does, it does because the geometry made it.

4.3 million M☉

Sagittarius A*, the black hole at the centre of our own galaxy. Chosen because tidal force at the horizon falls off as 1/M², so a hole this big lets you cross the boundary without noticing — the stretch across a human body there is roughly a ten-thousandth of Earth gravity. A stellar-mass hole would have shredded the observer long before it arrived.

Aberration

By the horizon the camera is moving at about 65% of light speed, and that changes what it sees more than the curvature does. Relativistic aberration sweeps the sky forward into a bunched, brightened cone, and the shadow gets smaller as you approach rather than swallowing the view. Falling in doesn't look like being engulfed. It looks like the universe closing behind you.

r = 0.55 M

Where the integration stops, and not because of a bug. Approaching the ring singularity, curvature genuinely diverges — momentum runs past a thousand and the conserved quantity starts to drift no matter how small the steps get. That's the mathematics failing, not the code. Everything past that point on screen is interpretation, and it's labelled as such.
